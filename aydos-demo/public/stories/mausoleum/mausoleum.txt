[1] The fire crackled in the small living room, casting long, dancing shadows across the walls. Oliver sat completely still on the rug, his eyes fixed on his grandfather. The old man peered into the glowing logs for a long moment before he began to speak, his voice dropping to a low, dramatic whisper. "It happened fifty years ago, Oliver, right on the highest hill of Blackwood Cemetery. I was twenty years old, walking home under a bright autumn moon when I froze in my tracks."

[2] "There, sitting where only empty grass stood during the day, was a beautiful, white stone building. I knew immediately it was a mausoleum — something built to both remember and hold the dead. But Oakhaven was just a simple little village. No family could have afforded such a thing.

[3]"I felt an overwhelming curiosity, so I crept past the iron gates. The air around the stone was freezing cold. I shone my torch on the walls and read the names carved into the rock. Arthur Pendelton, 1640. Clara Oswald, 1789. Peter Jenkins, 1895. None of them were Oakhaven names. They were completely unrelated families from entirely different centuries. They were total strangers." 

[4] "Weren't you scared, Grandpa?" Oliver whispered, his face pale.

The old man nodded slowly. "I was. But I soon came to learn the building only appeared during our darkest moments. My first sighting coincided with the night my own mother passed away. The second time, twenty years later, the village crops had failed, and we faced a bitter, freezing winter. The third time was the night your grandmother left this world. It only shows itself when my heart is heavy."

[5] "Do you think it’s a bad sign?" Oliver asked, shivering.

"I used to think so," his grandfather replied softly, looking out the dark window. "I thought it was a warning. But the building has never threatened me. In a strange way, it’s become a silent companion. It’s always there when I'm most alone."

[6] "The townsfolk laughed at me when I first spoke of it, so I decided never to tell another soul. It kept me apart from the village. But I’ve spent fifty years thinking about what it means. I believe that when my own time comes to leave this world, it will return one last time. And this time, those heavy marble doors will finally swing open for me."

[7] "I don’t know if I fear that day, or if I welcome it. It’s a frightening place to go, yes. But perhaps it’s also a quiet refuge for tired travellers who have nowhere left to turn."

The old man leaned forward, the firelight catching the serious expression on his lined face. "Because I know you saw it on your walk home this evening, didn't you? And I sense your heart is heavy tonight."
