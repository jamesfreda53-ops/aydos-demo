[1] Clara stood frozen in her living room, staring down at the shattered pieces of her grandfather's antique brass clock scattered across the wooden floorboards. If she knew anything about repairing vintage mechanical clocks, she would calmly gather the tiny gears and start fixing the delicate inner springs right now. She didn't know how to repair the clock, though. Her heart sank as she realized that she had no idea how to piece back together an intricate mechanism like that without a professional watchmaker's tools.

[2] If she hadn't left the heavy brass ornament resting so close to the edge of the low bookshelf, the disaster never would have happened in the first place. Of course, if her notoriously mischievous cat didn't treat every high surface in the apartment as his own personal runway, things would be much safer. Yakkai was always naughty, though, no matter how much she tried to get him to behave.

[3] She remembered how Yakkai had carelessly batted his paw at the swinging pendulum just minutes earlier, completely ignoring her urgent shouts. If she had only kept an eye on Yakkai while she was making tea, he wouldn't have done anything naughty, and the precious family heirloom wouldn't have fallen to the floor. It would still be ticking away, safely on display. Now, staring at the broken dial, she could only wish she possessed the steady hands of a master craftsman, while deeply regretting that she had not paid closer attention to her cat's reckless habits earlier that afternoon.

[4] Clara picked up her phone and searched for clock repair shops nearby. The first shop she found specialized in antique clocks, but its prices made her wince. If she had more money, she would take the broken clock there immediately. Unfortunately, her student budget was already stretched thin. If repairing old clocks were easier, she would try to fix it herself, but one look at the dozens of tiny gears scattered across the floor convinced her not to risk it. She carefully gathered the pieces and placed them in a small wooden box.

[5] Yakkai wandered over and sniffed the wooden box, apparently curious about the trouble he had caused. Clara quickly picked him up before he could make things worse. “If you were a better-behaved cat, my life would be much easier,” she told him. Yakkai stared back at her without the slightest sign of guilt. Clara sighed and looked again at the broken clock. If she had kept the cabinet door closed, none of this would have happened. She couldn't change the past, but at least she could keep Yakkai away from the pieces until she found someone who could repair them.
