[1] On certain nights of the year, when the moon is full and there are heavy clouds in the sky to ward off any wandering eyes from the heavens, the dead will dance come dusk. If you stop by at the right time, and keep your voice low, so as not to spook them, you too can watch them shake their old bones free of dust and earth. By the cemetery, where the air smells of moss and mud and fresh things sprouting without a gardener to prune them, you can take your place atop the hill, and peer downwards towards the graves. 

[2] You'll have to squint in that blue evening light, but you'll see them, skulls and femurs in bad need of a polish, clothes a century out of style and just holding on by a thread (they still try to guard their decency). You'll hear the creak and crack and croak of dry limbs and parched throats, the heavy, labored scrape of a tomb pushed open, a coffin lid tossed aside, and though they're clumsy, years out of practice, they'll dance as best they can. 

[3] Sometimes, they'll ask you to join them. It happens every so often. They'll catch sight of you, tucked away in the brush, and if they're in a good mood, and what's left of their joints isn't too sore, one of them might reach out a hand and beckon for you. Don't be afraid. They're harmless, really. They mean well. Take that hand and join the dance. They just want some company. It gets lonely down there in the earth. Sooner or later, you'll understand. 


[4] When the sun comes up, they'll see you off and wish you well. Don't linger about too long: it's rude. They've got to tuck themselves back in, before the day gets hot. They'll seal themselves up, to keep out the worms, and help each other fill in their graves. They're quite the clean and clever creatures. If someone comes by later to lay some flowers on a tomb, they won't notice anything amiss. They appreciate the flowers, by the way. They really do.
