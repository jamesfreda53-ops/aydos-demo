[1] The dressing room was completely silent, save for the hum of the air conditioner and the heavy sound of Mia's breathing. In just a few minutes, she was supposed to walk out onto the stage in front of eighty thousand screaming fans for the opening night of her world tour, the biggest of her career, or rather, the biggest so far, as her manager liked to add. But Mia could not move. Her heels stuck to the floor. Her heart pumped ice through her veins and into her limbs, making it impossible to draw a breath.

[2] Her hands trembled as she clutched the edge of the makeup table, staring into the warmly lit mirror. No fluorescents in the green room. That was in her contract.

For ten years, that glass barrier had been her most trusted confidante. Long before the sold-out stadiums and the platinum albums, back when she practiced singing alone in her damp basement, the figure in the glass had been there. It was the quiet companion who mirrored her exhaustion during late-night rehearsals, the silent partner with fierce eyes who nodded encouragement when the industry tried to break her spirit. The reflection's name was Mia Chrome. It took that name long before the whole world knew it. They had always shared an unspoken agreement across the divide.

Until tonight.

[3] The panic hit her like riptide, pulling her out to sea. Her heart hammered wildly against her ribs, the room began to dim, and the lights of the vanity tightened into sharp, threatening needles. You aren't losing your mind, someone whispered. Not tonight. Any night but tonight.

Mia squeezed her eyes shut to stop the dizziness. When she opened them again, the figure in the mirror was staring at her, perplexed. While she hunched over the table, gasping for oxygen, Mia Chrome stood perfectly rigid, posture immaculate, eyes wide and fixed. Mia's mascara was running. Mia Chrome's was not.

[4] She felt like she had mercury in her veins. The woman in the glass was not a reflection at all. It was an accumulation of every photoshoot, music video and magazine cover, every piece of herself she had traded away, slowly growing stronger over the years. It had waited patiently for this exact moment.

Mia screamed, but no sound came past her dry lips. Mia Chrome cocked her head. Save your breath, dear. Save your voice. Shall I take it from here? 

[5] In the mirror, the copy lifted a hand and pressed its fingertips flat against the glass from the inside. Solid silver condensation formed beneath its touch. Mia reached out a trembling hand, her own fingertips meeting the surface from the outside. Mia Chrome pushed her manicured hand through the glass, and Mia pulled, very gently. 

[6] Her double crept out of the mirror with astonishing grace. Not a hair out of place. And the costume she'd been so worried about looked perfect on her. Bold, but tasteful, and just the right shade. Mia felt tiny compared to her. That dour, chilly dressing room was scarcely big enough to fit Mia Chrome. Shhh, it said to her. You just wait in here. I'll only be a moment. 

[7] There was a knock at the door. 

"Mia! You're on. Don't make them wait!" her manager shouted. Mia Chrome stood, checked her reflection one more time. Perfect. She was perfect. 

"Coming," she said. "I wouldn't miss this for the world." 

She left the lights on in her dressing room. In that empty room, lipstick smeared, looking a right mess in that ridiculous costume, there was still a figure in the mirror. It was better this way. After all, no one had come tonight to see her.
