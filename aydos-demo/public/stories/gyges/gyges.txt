[1] Candaules of the Heraclidae, king of Lydia, was a man sick with pride, convinced his wife’s beauty was a masterpiece to be marveled at. Every night, when the wine had loosened his tongue, he boasted of her perfection to his most trusted bodyguard, Gyges.

"You must look upon her, Gyges," the king urged. "A man only believes what his own eyes witness."

Gyges, however, refused, knowing this was a great crime. "Sire, I beg of you. Do not ask this of me. It violates the laws of gods and men alike."

"Nonsense," Candaules laughed. "I shall hide you behind the bedchamber door. She will never know."

[2] There was no arguing with a king. That very night, Gyges slipped into the royal chamber, his heart hammering against his ribs. From behind the ornately carved door, he watched through a sliver of space as Queen Nysia entered.

Nysia unpinned her delicate gold clasps, letting garment by garment fall to the floor. Gyges squeezed his eyes shut in shame, but curiosity pried them open again—just in time to see the stiffening of the queen’s shoulders. Her eyes had caught his reflection in the polished bronze mirror.

[3] A terrible stillness fell over the room. Nysia did not gasp or scream. Instead, her reflection stared straight into the eyes of the hidden guard. With terrifying, deliberate calm, she finished undressing and walked to the bed. Gyges fled into the night, sick to his stomach, knowing a death sentence awaited him.

[4] By dawn, as Gyges awaited punishment for his crime, a royal summons arrived from Queen Nysia herself. He was escorted not to the throne room, but directly into Nysia’s private chambers. The air was heavy, scented with incense and fine perfume. Nysia sat upon a high-backed chair, draped in red silk, her dark eyes piercing through her veil. She did not look upon him with contempt, though. She eyed him up and down, assessing his usefulness. 

"You were my little ghost last night, Gyges," Nysia said, her voice a low, venomous hiss that echoed off the stone. “The King thought he could toss away my honor like a common merchant hawking his wares. That fool has signed his own death warrant, and you, little ghost, shall be the pen."

[5] Gyges fell to his knees, pressing his forehead against the cold floor. "My queen, I was commanded—I begged him—"
"Silence!" Nysia rose, her eyes flashing with unforgiving fire. “You were his accomplice. You chose to look. Now, you have two paths, and only two. You will face your execution, and your name shall become synonymous with disgrace…” She paused for a moment. “Or, you will kill the man who betrayed me, seize his throne, and take me as your wife. Choose. His life, or yours.” 

The silence in the room was absolute. Gyges slowly raised his head, looking up at this woman who commanded both fear and absolute awe. The crown of Lydia hung in the balance. His decision, Gyges realized, had already been made. 

[6] That night, as Candaules lay in bed, drowsy from the drugged wine the Queen had given him, Gyges stepped out of the shadows, and took Nysia's place in bed beside the king. He drew his silver dagger, and with a decisive strike, brought an end to the king's madness forever.

[7] News of Gyges' actions spread throughout Lydia, and many took up arms, ready to depose the usurper. But Gyges declared that they should first consult the Oracle at Delphi. If she ordained him king of Lydia, he would reign, and if not, he would return the kingship to the Heraclidae. 

The Oracle confirmed his rule, and thus did Gyges bring an end to the reign of the Heraclidae. As king, he sent many offerings of silver and gold to the Oracle, and these fine gifts still reside at Delphi today.
